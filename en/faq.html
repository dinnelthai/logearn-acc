Getting StartedHow do I buy Credits?

Credits are the usage fee for the AI strategy engine. You buy them on the Use Credits panel on the "API Key Management" page, in two steps.

Step 1: Top up. In the top-right of the panel, click 充值 (Top Up), choose the Solana chain + SOL, and transfer 0.5 SOL to the wallet address that pops up. Note the warning: this address only supports Solana mainnet deposits — do not transfer cross-chain.

Top-up dialog: choose the chain, choose SOL, then transfer to the address below the QR code.

Step 2: Purchase. Once the funds arrive, click 购买 100K @ 0.5 SOL (Buy 100K @ 0.5 SOL) on the right side of the panel — 0.5 SOL buys 100K Credits.

The green button pointed to by the red arrow is the purchase entry point; you can adjust the quantity on the left.

How long does 100K last? Click Credits 账单 (Credits Billing) in the top-left of the panel to see line-by-line consumption records (strategy usage fees). Based on actual usage: running a single 1.5-segment strategy, 100K lasts about 4 days; running a single Wake-up Signal strategy lasts about 5 days. Running multiple strategies shortens this proportionally, so remember to top up before you run out.

Consumption billing: each entry is a strategy usage fee, ranging from a few to over a hundred Credits.

TroubleshootingThe candlestick chart looks wrong, the holder-distribution bar is off, or indicators display incorrectly — what do I do?

First check the two display toggles at the top. Holder distribution only supports the USD + 市值 (Market Cap) combination — switching to SOL pricing or 价格 (Price) basis can cause the candlestick chart, holder-distribution bar, and indicators to all break.

The toggles are to the right of the timeframe row: USD / SOL and 价格 / 市值 (Price / Market Cap). If something looks wrong, switch them back to USD and 市值 (Market Cap) first — that fixes it in most cases.

The red box marks the USD / SOL and 价格 / 市值 (Price / Market Cap) toggles.

TroubleshootingThe holder-distribution bar suddenly disappeared entirely?

It's most likely because you clicked log in the bottom-right corner. Once the chart switches to a logarithmic scale, the candlestick chart displays incorrectly and the holder-distribution bar on the right simply disappears.

log sits in the bottom-right of the chart, to the right of the timestamp, next to % and 自动 (Auto). Click it again to toggle it off and everything comes back.

This button is easy to hit by accident while zooming the chart — if the holder-distribution bar vanishes, check here first.

TroubleshootingAn indicator disappeared, or the chart panes above/below got scrambled?

Check whether you accidentally touched the three dots to the right of a header. The candlestick chart and every indicator header (e.g. "Volume WMA 1") has a ⋯ menu after it, and two items in that menu can mess up your chart:

  • 隐藏 (Hide) — immediately hides that candlestick series or indicator, so it disappears from the chart entirely.
  • The move up/down options inside 移动到 (Move To) — moves the indicator to a different pane, scrambling the top-to-bottom order of the main chart and sub-charts.

"隐藏" (Hide) and "移除" (Remove) sit next to each other in the menu, so a slip of the hand can easily hit the wrong one. The "对象树… (Object Tree…)" option in the menu lists everything currently on the chart — check there if something has gone missing.

If you're not sure, don't click around randomly in this menu — use the row at the top to change the display basis instead of touching the header.

TroubleshootingThe candlestick chart is invisible, indicators are wrong, and none of the above fixes work?

The ultimate fix: clear the local cache. The display state of the candlestick chart and indicators is stored locally in your browser — once that cache gets corrupted, no amount of toggling will save it. Clearing it and reloading fixes it.

The option lives behind the gear icon (Settings) on the right of the top bar: open it, find "清空本地缓存" (Clear Local Cache), click the red 清空 (Clear) button, then refresh the page.

Top bar gear icon → Clear Local Cache → 清空 (Clear) — the two red arrows mark these spots.

Signal SystemWhat exactly are the six signals?

Early Pick, Wake-up Signal, Post-Pullback Rebound Signal, Wallets I Follow, Blue-chip Top Winner Wallet Resonance, and X (Twitter) Lead Car.

You can enable all six, or keep just one. When only one is kept, the signal list becomes a dedicated dashboard for that signal type.

Signal SystemHow do I view just one signal type?

Click the "Signal Type" dropdown at the top and uncheck the other five, leaving only the one you want. The number to the right of the dropdown updates accordingly — for example, it shows 1 when only one is kept.

Signal SystemWhat's the difference between "Early Pick Count (24H)" and "Time Since Latest Pick (min)"?

One controls frequency, the other controls freshness — they're only meaningful when used together.

  • 早期精选次数(24H) (Early Pick Count (24H)) — set a minimum value: how many times it was picked in the last 24 hours; a higher count means more sustained attention.
  • 最新精选距今时长(分) (Time Since Latest Pick (min)) — set a maximum value: how long ago it was last picked; setting 60 means only within the last hour.

Filtering by count alone without capping the time will surface a bunch of coins that were hot yesterday but have already cooled off.

Dashboard SetupHow do I bring up the trending list?

Two steps: keep all six signal types selected, then click the sort icon in the top-right of the list and choose 24小时热度 (24H Heat) under "Sort by Token".

This is what the community usually calls the trending list — it doesn't care about signal type, it just shows whichever coins are hottest overall right now.

Dashboard SetupWhat does the green number on the filter dialog's tab label mean?

It's how many conditions have been set under that tab, not how many coins were matched. A lot of people misread this the first time.

For example, if the War Room dashboard's "Migrated" tab shows 5, that breaks down as: 1 condition under Launch Platform + 2 under Featured Metrics + 2 under General Metrics. After filling everything in, check these numbers against each other to make sure you haven't missed anything.

The War Room's three stages (New / About to Migrate / Migrated) are each counted independently and don't affect one another.

Dashboard SetupWhat's a good value for token age?

The unit is minutes, entered in the "Max" field. Two common presets:

  • 1440 = 24 hours — shows only coins from today; used for the second-stage dashboard.
  • 500 ≈ 8 hours — shows only coins that just launched; used for the War Room dashboard.
Dashboard SetupCan I save my filter settings to reuse next time?

Yes. The bottom of the filter dialog has "Template", "Import", "Export", and "Save". "Confirm" applies the filter for this session only; "Save" stores it as a template you can load directly next time.

Once you've got a setup you like, it's best to save it right away — otherwise you'll have to re-enter everything when you switch dashboards and come back.

Dashboard SetupWhat if the filtered results are too few / too many?

The parameters aren't fixed — adjust them in this direction:

  • Too few results — lower the minimum on "count"-type fields, and widen the maximum on "time since" fields.
  • Too much noise — tighten them the other way.

It's best to run the tutorial's default values for a day first, get a feel for it, then fine-tune at your own pace.

Risk ManagementHow much junk-wallet holding is considered safe?

This is a hard rule. Genuine moonshots are almost always under 1%; anything over 5% can basically be skipped outright, without even bothering to check the chart.

The two values given in the tutorial already include a margin: the second-stage signal dashboard tightens it to 5, while the War Room dashboard relaxes it to 10 — at the War Room stage, the pool hasn't been fully cleaned up yet, so the bar is set a bit looser, but that's as far as it goes.

Risk ManagementIs a high new-wallet holding automatically a bad sign?

Not necessarily. Newly launched coins naturally have a lot of new wallets, so this metric alone isn't conclusive.

It's a soft threshold: above 70% you should be cautious, since it's often a sign of a coordinated scheme run by a batch of throwaway accounts. But it only becomes reliable when read together with junk-wallet holding — high new-wallet with low junk-wallet may just mean the coin is new; both being high is what you actually want to avoid.

Risk ManagementWhat do all those wallet-holding percentages on the right side mean?

In the "Featured Metrics" panel on the right of the token detail page, this is a breakdown of the coin's holder structure: high-frequency wallets, new wallets, old wallets, blue-chip top winner wallets, smart-money wallets, junk wallets, AMM wallets, and exchange wallets.

These numbers are the core basis for judging whether the pool is clean, and they're exactly what the "Featured Metrics" tab in the filter dialog lets you filter on.

UI OperationsWhere do I open the signal dashboard and the War Room dashboard?

Both are in the global entry bar along the bottom row of the token detail page: Watchlist, Signals, Trending, Monitor, Positions, War Room, AI Strategy. Click "Signals" to open the signal dashboard, click "War Room" to open the War Room dashboard — no need to go back to the home page.

UI OperationsWhy do the filter dialogs for the signal dashboard and the War Room dashboard look different?

Because they filter different things — don't apply one dashboard's mental model to the other.

  • Signal dashboard: the top section is "Signal Filter", centered on the count and time-since fields for the six signals.
  • War Room dashboard: the top section is the three stages "New / About to Migrate / Migrated", each with its own conditions — there's no signal-count-style field here.

What they share are the Launch Platform, Featured Metrics, and General Metrics sections.

UI OperationsWhat do the War Room's "New / About to Migrate / Migrated" stages each mean?

Three stages split by the token's lifecycle:

  • New — just launched, still on the internal order book. The noisiest stage.
  • About to Migrate — the internal order book is nearly full and about to move to the external market. The position to stake out ahead of time.
  • Migrated — already graduated to the external market, liquidity has picked up, and there's more data to base a judgment on.
