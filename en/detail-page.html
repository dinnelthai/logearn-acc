Using the Product

Token Detail Page Layout

Click into any token and this is the page you land on. Signal stream on the left, K-line chart in the middle, order panel on the right, function tabs at the bottom — first get clear on where each region is, then see how the two hands-on dashboards are set up: the Stage-2 Signal dashboard and the Trenches dashboard.

The Page Is Split Into Four Regions

Left to right, top to bottom — that's all the regions on the detail page.

1

Left · Live Signal Stream

A continuously scrolling signal list. Each entry shows the token name, market cap, number of signals, time elapsed, and the max gain since the first alert. Click any entry to jump to that token's detail page. The whole column can be collapsed from the top-left corner.

2

Middle · Main K-Line Chart

Timeframes range from 1s to 1d; the second-level timeframes are for chasing fast-moving tokens. VWAP is overlaid on the chart, with volume and two AO momentum indicators below it. The top-right corner toggles between Price / Market Cap and USD / SOL display modes.

⚠ These two toggles only render the chip distribution chart correctly when set to USD + Market Cap. Switching away from that combination will make the K-line, chip distribution peaks, and indicators all behave incorrectly.

3

Right · Trading Panel + Holder Structure

The top half covers buy/sell, market/limit orders, quick amounts, and take-profit/stop-loss. The "Featured Metrics" section in the bottom half shows this token's wallet holder structure — how much is held by high-frequency traders, new wallets, old wallets, blue-chip top winners, smart money, and junk wallets. These figures are the core basis for judging whether a token's holder base is clean.

4

Bottom · Function Tabs + Global Entry Points

The tab bar has Trade Details, Signal Analysis, DCA/Limit Analysis, Positions, Open Orders, Trade History, and My Strategies. The very bottom row is the global entry bar: Watchlist, Signals, Hot, Monitor, Positions, Trenches, AI Strategy — the Signal dashboard opens right from here.

There are three known chart gotchas — if "the chart looks wrong," check these first:
1. Chip distribution only works with USD + Market Cap — switching to SOL or Price mode will make the K-line, chip distribution peaks, and indicators all behave incorrectly.
2. The log (logarithmic axis) button in the bottom-right corner makes the chip distribution peaks disappear entirely, and the K-line will also display incorrectly. It's easy to click by accident while zooming the chart — just click it again to undo.
3. Don't click carelessly on the ⋯ menu next to each header — it appears behind the K-line header and every indicator's header. "Hide" inside it removes that item from the chart entirely, and the up/down options under "Move To" will reorder the panes between the main chart and sub-charts. "Hide" sits right next to "Remove," too.

Gotcha 1: Display Mode Must Be USD + Market Cap

The two toggles sit to the right of the timeframe row. The red arrows point to the state you need to keep: USD and Market Cap. Switch to SOL or Price and the chip distribution chart won't render.

Gotcha 3: The ⋯ Menu on Each Header

Move up/down → pane order gets scrambled
Hide → gone from the chart instantly

A ⋯ menu hangs off the K-line header and every indicator header. "Hide" removes it from the chart right away; the up/down options under "Move To" will reorder the panes between the main chart and sub-charts — and "Remove" sits right below "Hide," so one shaky click and it's gone.

Bottom Global Entry Points

No need to go back to the home page — the bottom row of the detail page switches dashboards directly. The two most-used ones are "Signals" and "Trenches."

1"Signals" — Signal Dashboard

信号 (Signals) is the leftmost item in the bottom row. Click it to open the Signal dashboard, where the filtering, sorting, and screening for all six signal types is configured. See Custom Filtering for the Six Signals → for setup instructions.

2"Trenches" — Trenches Dashboard

战壕 (Trenches), further along the same row — click it and the left column switches to the Trenches list, split into three stages: 新创建 (Newly Created) / 即将迁移 (About to Migrate) / 已迁移 (Migrated).

Sub-Topic: The Stage-2 Signal Dashboard

Find tokens that spiked past $150K within the last 24 hours, have since pulled back, but are still holding above $50K — a "stage-2" runner that already had its first move and hasn't died yet.

The Idea

"Stage 1" is the run from zero to the initial peak, which most people miss. "Stage 2" is betting there's a second wave after the pullback from that peak. So the conditions need to lock down three things at once: it really did spike (all-time-high market cap ≥ 150K), it's still alive (current market cap ≥ 50K), and it's still fresh enough (token age ≤ 24 hours). Then use two wallet-structure metrics to filter out manipulated and junk tokens.

Standard Metrics

Current mkt cap ≥ 50 · ATH ≥ 150
Token age ≤ 1440

In "Filter Settings" → Standard Metrics tab, fill in three fields.

Once all six fields are filled in, the "Signal Filter" indicator at the top of the popup will show 6, the Launch Platform row will show Clear Selection (3), and the "Featured Metrics" and "Standard Metrics" tabs will show 2 and 3 respectively — matching these numbers confirms nothing was missed. Click "Confirm" to apply, or "Save" to store it as a template you can load later.
Junk wallet holdings is a hard cutoff: once it's above 5%, you can basically skip the token without even checking the chart. New wallet holdings at 70% is a soft threshold — it's only reliable when read alongside junk wallets, since a high new-wallet share alone isn't necessarily bad (freshly launched tokens naturally have lots of new wallets).

Sub-Topic: The Trenches Dashboard

Stage-2 waits for a token to finish a full run before getting in. Trenches camps out before the token even starts running. The filter popups for the two dashboards look completely different — don't try to reuse the Signal dashboard's setup here.

Three Stages, Three Sets of Conditions

Trenches splits tokens into three stages by lifecycle, and those are the three tabs at the top of the popup. Each stage has its own filter conditions — whichever stage tab you're on, what you fill in below applies only to that stage.

1

Newly Created

Tokens that just launched and are still on the internal bonding-curve market. This stage is the noisiest — whether you can find anything worthwhile depends entirely on your filter conditions.

2

About to Migrate

Tokens whose internal market is nearly full and about to move to the external market. This is the stage for getting positioned ahead of time.

3

Migrated

Tokens that have already graduated to the external market. Liquidity has picked up, and there's more data to judge by. The configuration below is set on this stage.

The number on each tab = how many conditions have been set for that stage, not how many tokens matched. In the screenshot, "Migrated" shows 5, which breaks down as 1 condition for Launch Platform + 2 for Featured Metrics + 2 for Standard Metrics; "About to Migrate" shows 1, meaning only one condition is currently set for that stage. The numbers for all three stages are independent of each other.

Standard Metrics

Current mkt cap ≥ 30
Token age ≤ 500

Switch to "Migrated," then in the Standard Metrics tab fill in two fields: current market cap min 30, token age max 500.

Featured Metrics

New wallets ≤ 70 · Junk wallets ≤ 10

In the Featured Metrics tab, fill in two wallet-structure fields: new wallet holdings max 70, junk wallet holdings max 10.

These two wallet metrics are an extra filtering layer you're adding yourself, not a hard rule from the platform — loosen them if you feel you're missing too much, tighten them if you feel there's too much junk getting through. Junk wallets is set to 10 in Trenches and tightened to 5 in Stage-2 simply because the two dashboards target different stages of a token's life.
