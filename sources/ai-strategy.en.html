AI Strategy Engine

The strategy engine surfaces in several places in the product — the top navigation, the bottom global bar, the detail-page tab, and the strategy dropdown in the filter popup. Get familiar with the entry points first, then learn how to configure it.

Four Places It Shows Up

It's the same underlying feature, but there are four entry points in the UI — don't get them confused.

1

Top Navigation · AI Strategy Engine

On the right side of the top bar, next to API and VIP. This is the main entry point for the strategy engine.

2

Bottom Global Bar · AI Strategy

On the same row as "Watchlist / Signals / Hot / Monitor / Positions / Trenches", at the far right. You can open it from the detail page without leaving it.

3

Detail-Page Tab · My Strategies

In the tab bar in the lower half of the detail page, alongside Trade Details, Signal Analysis, DCA/Limit Analysis, Positions, Open Orders, and Trade History. The number in parentheses is how many are currently active.

4

Filter Popup · AI Strategy Dropdown

Both the Signal dashboard and the Trenches dashboard have an AI Strategy field in their "Filter Settings," defaulting to No Strategy Limit. This lets you filter down to only the tokens matched by a specific strategy. See Custom Filtering for the Six Signal Types → for setup details.

The Three Official Strategies

The engine ships with three official strategies preloaded, all live and running with real trading data. You can treat them purely as signals, or turn on automated trading orders directly.

11.5-Stage Strategy · Strong-Market Strategy

Each strategy is one full block: a bar chart of historical hits on the left, returns and win-rate distribution in the middle, and the six-layer filter counts plus the run/pause toggle on the right.

2Wake-up Relay Strategy

This one has 自动交易 (Auto Trading) checked — once it hits, there's no manual order needed, it's placed automatically.

The Wake-up Relay Strategy is best paired with grid orders. Set the buy method to 网格挂单 (Grid Order), the reference price to 当前市值 (Current Market Cap), and the drawdown range to 30% ~ 100%. This way, a hit doesn't trigger a single market buy — instead it lays down limit orders in batches across the drawdown range from market cap, averaging down the cost basis.
1Wake-up Strategy Order Parameters

Grid Order + Current Market Cap + drawdown range 30% ~ 100%.

Core of the Wake-up Strategy: Sustained 1-Minute Volume

The Wake-up Relay Strategy puts heavy weight on whether 1-minute trading volume is sustained. A rising price is only the surface — whether volume underneath keeps up is the real key to deciding whether to hold or run.

Core principle: if the price rises but volume doesn't keep up, run immediately; if the first volume spike is huge but subsequent volume collapses quickly, that's also dangerous. Only rising price + sustained volume expansion is worth holding and watching.

Importing Official Strategies

No need to configure every parameter by hand. The official team exported the three main strategies as text — paste it in and it just works.

1

Get the Strategy Text

Go to github.com/logearn/logearn-ai-strategies, open import.txt, and copy the whole content.

2

Click "Import" in the Top-Right

On the "My Strategies" page, at the far right of the top bar, next to + 新增策略 (+ New Strategy).

3

Paste, Then Click "Import"

The popup has just one input box, with a placeholder that reads "Paste exported strategy text." Paste it in, click the green 导入 (Import) button, and the strategy appears in the list — all parameters come across at once, no manual setup needed.

1Import Entry Point

The 导入 (Import) button at the right of the top bar, where the red arrow points.

2Import Popup

One input box plus two buttons — nothing else.

3Copy the Strategy Code

Go to github.com/logearn/logearn-ai-strategies, open code.js, and copy the whole content into "Strategy Logic."

Import is the reverse of "Export." Every strategy card has an 导出 (Export) button in its top-right corner; clicking it generates a block of text — that's exactly what you paste here. So besides the official strategies, this is also how people share strategies with each other in the community.

The Basic Settings Page

After importing, all parameters are already carried over — this page is mainly for checking and fine-tuning. The two things you'll typically actually change are the active time window and the buy order amount.

1Top Half · Trigger Method

The four tabs at the top are 基本设置 (Basic Settings), 策略逻辑 (Strategy Logic), 实时日志 (Live Logs), and 清理 (Cleanup); the top right has 保存策略 (Save Strategy) and AI 生成代码 (AI-Generate Code). You must click save after making changes.

2Bottom Half · Trading and Tiers

From the active time window downward: the Auto Trading toggle, order method, amount, wallet, take-profit/stop-loss, and win-rate distribution tiers.

Strategy Trigger Method (Choose One of Three)

This is the single most important choice on the page — it determines which data this strategy can use.

Method How It Triggers / What Data It Can Use
Signal-Triggered Driven by signals already on the platform — good for adding one more filter layer on top of a signal. Can use all data: logearn holders kline indicator chip_analysis. All three official strategies use this method.
Token Live-Stream-Triggered Covers every token's every-second changes across the whole chain — good for "chain scanning" strategies (new tokens / just graduated / market cap just crossed a threshold). Can only use logearn and signals data — no access to holders or chip analysis.
Strategy-Triggered Subscribes to the pool of matched tokens produced by one or more "Token Live Stream" strategies, then re-checks tokens in that pool using richer data. Can use all data — effectively, the live stream does a rough first pass, and this does the fine filtering.

Trading Parameters

Parameter Description
Enable Auto Trading Automatically buys on a hit. Unchecked, it only produces a strategy signal without placing an order. Turn this off if you want to observe for a while first.
Allow Multiple Concurrent Positions Unchecked: no matter how many strategies you have running at once, the same token is bought only once, unless that position is sold and a new signal comes in. Checked: this check is skipped, allowing you to add to the same token's position repeatedly.
Buy Only on First Match Checked: this token is bought only once — after selling, even if this strategy matches it again, it won't be bought again. Unchecked: only checks whether you currently hold a position — no buy if you're holding, and buys again if you're not holding and a new signal comes in.
Buy Order Method 市价买入 (Market Buy): buys at market price the instant it hits. 网格挂单 (Grid Order): doesn't buy immediately, and instead lays down limit orders in batches — configuration is in the next section. Once switched to Grid Order, the "Buy Order Amount" above no longer applies; the amount is instead determined by the grid template.
Buy Order Amount How much to buy each time under Market Buy, followed by the quote currency: USDC / USD1 / USDT / SOL. The screenshot shows 10 SOL.
Trading Wallet Which wallet places the order — the balance is shown on the right. Always double-check this after importing someone else's strategy — the wallet and amount are tied to your own account.

Grid Orders

Switching "Buy Order Method" to 网格挂单 (Grid Order) reveals an extra block of settings below, and the "Buy Order Amount" field above changes to "Trading Currency."

What Grid Order looks like expanded. The red text above marks the behavior when "Buy Only on First Match" is unchecked — no buy while holding, buys when there's no position and a signal arrives.

Parameter Description
How Orders Are Placed Instead of buying at market price immediately on a hit, it places batches of limit buy orders across the drawdown range from the reference price, according to the template. Selling still follows the take-profit/stop-loss configured above.
Trading Currency USDC / USD1 / USDT / SOL. The amount configured in the template (in USD or native token) is converted to this currency at the current exchange rate before the order is placed.
Reference Price 当前市值 (Current Market Cap) or 最高市值 (Highest Market Cap). The drawdown range is calculated relative to this price — choosing Highest Market Cap means orders are laid down from the historical high, so the same range places orders lower.
Drawdown Range Two percentages; the screenshot shows -0% to -50%, meaning grid levels are laid out across the range from the reference price down to half of it.
Grid Template Determines how much each order buys and how many levels are laid out; the screenshot shows 买$10平分网格挂单(5 格) (Buy $10, split evenly across a grid order (5 levels)). The per-order buy amount comes from the template, not the "Buy Order Amount" field above — click the gear icon next to it to change the template's amount.
Weighing Grid Orders vs. Market Buy. Market Buy takes the fill the moment a signal arrives, prioritizing speed; Grid Orders wait for a pullback and buy in batches, prioritizing cost basis — but the trade-off is that if the token just rips upward, none of the grid levels fill. Signals like Post-pullback Rebound are already about buying dips, so grid pairs well with them; something like Early Pick that needs to move fast can end up empty-handed with a grid.
Check two things first after importing. First, the active time window — someone else's exported strategy may run on their schedule, so reset it to your own timezone and monitoring hours. Second, the trading wallet and buy order amount — these are tied to your own account, and skipping this check while enabling auto trading means trading on default values.

Strategy Logic: Writing the Decision Code

Basic Settings controls "when it triggers and how orders are placed on a hit"; Strategy Logic controls "whether you actually want it once it hits." This is a block of JavaScript — the platform loads data into ctx, and your code decides whether to let a token through or exclude it.

Pasting code replaces the whole block — it doesn't append. The editor always holds exactly one script. When you get new code, select all and delete the existing content before pasting — appending it below leaves two blocks of logic stacked on top of each other, which at best duplicates checks and at worst throws errors outright. Remember to click 保存策略 (Save Strategy) in the top right after editing.

The Strategy Logic tab. The row at the top shows this strategy's most recently matched tokens; below it is the code editor.

Common Patterns in the Code

Pattern What It Does
return false Excludes this token — it doesn't match. Every "veto" block in the screenshot returns false as soon as it finds a problem.
ctx.log.error(...) Writes a log line, viewable in the 实时日志 (Live Logs) tab. It's worth logging the reason and the actual values while excluding a token — otherwise once it's live you won't know why something didn't match.
|| {} and Array.isArray(...) A safety fallback before reading data. Some data sources aren't available under certain trigger methods (see the table below), and without a fallback this throws an error outright.

What's Attached to ctx

Data Source What's in It
ctx.chip_analysis Chip/holder distribution analysis. The screenshot uses above_percent, below_percent, and top5_holders (an array of the top five holders).
ctx.holders The holder list, an array — you can slice(0, 30) to get the Top 30. Each entry has address, total_hold_percent (share of holdings), and transfer_in_percent (share transferred in).
ctx.logearn LogEarn's own data; the screenshot uses token_address, mcap (current market cap), and max_up_mcap (all-time-high market cap).
ctx.kline_and_indicators K-line and technical indicators.
ctx.gmgn Data from GMGN.
Which data you can access depends on the trigger method. Signal-Triggered and Strategy-Triggered can use everything; Token Live-Stream-Triggered only has logearn and signals — writing ctx.holders or ctx.chip_analysis in a live-stream strategy gets you nothing, so those || {} fallback lines can't be skipped.

What the Code in the Screenshot Does

The official code is a classic "veto" pattern — it checks one thing after another, excluding a token as soon as it trips a red flag; only if it clears every check does it count as a match.

Check Logic
Blacklisted Address If a blacklisted address shows up in the Top 30 holders, exclude the token immediately.
Single Wallet Holding Too Much If any single wallet in the Top 5 by holdings has a share > 10%, exclude the token — this guards against a whale dumping.
Where the Top Holders' Tokens Came From If any Top 5 address has a transferred-in share > 5%, exclude the token. This requires the top holders' positions to have come mainly from buying, since a high transferred-in share suggests distribution or a rat-trading wallet.
Breaking Below the 0.86 Fib Level The low is fixed at 0, the high is the all-time-high market cap max_up_mcap, so the 0.86 retracement level = max_up_mcap × 0.14. If the current market cap breaks below this line, the token is blacklisted. It only looks at the current market cap, not the whole K-line history, so it won't wrongly exclude a token just because it "once broke below" that level in the past.
A gotcha built right into the code comments. The official team flagged the breakdown-blacklist block with a ⚠️: add_blacklist is a no-op under pure "Token Live Stream" mode — the code runs without erroring, but the blacklist entry is never actually written. If you're using Live-Stream-Triggered, go check the live logs after going live to confirm whether it's actually taking effect.

Real Trading Cases

Real cases where the strategy fired, reviewed after the fact. More to be added over time. Historical cases — not investment advice.

1Wake-up Strategy Hit · LAYOOO

Flow: Wake-up strategy catches the anomaly → checking X shows the community just kicked off a CTO → don't chase the first wave, wait for a pullback to get filled → combine the signal with the event for a manual entry decision. Strategy logged roughly 10.24x.

Take: this case is a clean fit for the Wake-up Strategy's core judgment call — "dead, then revived." First check that it's been dead long enough (a long stretch of low-volume, dormant sideways action), then wait for a CTO move at the moment of revival (community takeover, restarted operations) as the confirming signal. Stacking both conditions is more reliable than jumping in on volume alone.
2Wake-up Strategy Hit · CZ26

Flow: long stretch of dead, no-volume silence → a Twitter influencer posts a meme about a "Ferrari CZ26 custom supercar" tagging @cz_binance, with the replies full of people betting on whether CZ is making a comeback → the Wake-up strategy catches the anomaly at the same time, flagging two wake-up signals → the rally confirms once the influencer's topic gains traction.

Take: same underlying logic as case 1 — dead long enough is the precondition, and an external event/topic picking it back up at the moment of revival (this time not a CTO, but a Twitter influencer meme driving attention and CZ-comeback bets) is the confirming signal. The event type can vary, but the structure — "dormant first, then re-hyped" — stays the same. The strategy fires first, the event confirms it afterward.
